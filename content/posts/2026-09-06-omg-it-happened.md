## When the Side Project Hits: Celebration + Panic

Someone on r/sideproject just shouted from the rooftops: *“OMG IT HAPPENED!!!”* They didn’t specify what exactly happened, but we all know the vibe. Maybe it’s their first $10 on Gumroad. Maybe their product just got hunted on Product Hunt. Maybe their indie SaaS scored a paying customer after months of the launch crickets. Whatever the flavor, this is the moment when it starts to feel real.

And let’s be honest: we don’t talk enough about what comes *after* this “OMG moment.” Because the journey doesn’t stop here—if anything, things are about to get wild.

## What the Community Says About "The Moment"

**u/coffeeandcode42** nailed the essence in the thread: *“I made my first sale last night on a $9/mo plan, and I didn’t sleep because now I feel like I owe this person the world.”* This is peak Indie Hacker anxiety. You spend so long chasing someone, *anyone,* willing to pay for your janky little MVP—and then BAM, when it happens, imposter syndrome smacks you in the face.

Another user, **u/sandwichgeek**, shared what has to be my favorite analogy: *“It feels like your dog just licked a plate and called it fine dining—and someone PAID YOU for that.”*

## Screwing It Up After “OMG”

Here’s where it gets dicey, and several folks chimed in with stories of tripping after the first big win.

### Overreacting and Overbuilding

The most common trap people fall into? Overbuilding. After you hit your first milestone, it’s super tempting to turn into the over-engineering goblin. *“I need to rewrite this in Rust!”* or *“I should add login with OAuth!”*

Listen: **this is overkill for most people.** Your early adopter didn’t sign up for a polished beast—they signed up for the specific, laser-focused thing you offer right now. As **u/why0why** put it: *“The things you think are missing are often the exact features no one cares about.”*

**Case in point**: one user admitted to spending two weeks adding Stripe localization support after their first sale from Germany, only to never get another German customer again. Don’t overfit to edge cases.

### Chasing Channels That Don’t Work

Some folks also make the mistake of immediately throwing money at Google Ads or diving headfirst into the nearest SEO course. As **u/codeorangutan** wisely said: *“Your next step isn’t ‘scale,’ it’s doubling down on one thing that has traction.”*

If that first user found you through your Reddit post? That’s gold. Post more (and don’t spam). If it was a Tweet? Engage more on Twitter. Scaling comes later.

## What SHOULD You Do After the Moment?

Here’s the distilled takeaway from 50+ comments:

### 1. Talk to the People Who Paid
First real customer? Email them. DM them. Call them, if it’s not weird. u/shippingnerd put it like this: *“I asked my first two paying customers how they found me and why they signed up. Both said the demo video hooked them—so I doubled down on video.”*

Your early users are your best marketing data. Use it.

### 2. Keep Shipping, But Small Updates
You don’t need to build *all the things.* Just make the *one thing* slightly easier, faster, or less annoying. Add value, not bloat. As one user joked: *“Nobody canceled Netflix because the color scheme on one button sucked.”*

### 3. Maintain Momentum, But Spare Your Sanity
Don’t spiral into overwork just because you feel like you’re “on the clock” now. Popular comment from u/lazydogdev: *“I pulled three all-nighters after my first Gumroad sale. Then I got sick and didn’t launch anything for months. Don’t. Do. This.”*

Momentum > burnout. You gotta pace yourself because this is a marathon, not a sprint to $100 MRR.

## Real Examples, Reminder That You’re Not Alone

This thread actually got me thinking—who else had great OMG moments? A couple of classics to keep you inspired: 

- The [maker of Transistor.fm](https://transistor.fm/) (now a podcasting giant) felt their first OMG moment when *five people* signed up for their beta. Not hundreds. Five.
- Pieter Levels (yeah, *that Levels*) has publicly admitted he thought RemoteOK was worthless for MONTHS after launch.
- And let’s not forget **anyone** getting their indie product on HN’s front page—it’s terrifying, not glamorous. Eight spam signups, server crashes, and one random guy emailing you “this is bad and you should feel bad.”

These stories all started with the same jittery milestone as yours.

## Final Thoughts: It Happened, Now What?

“IT HAPPENED” is a thrilling thing to yell into the void. But it’s also just one data point in a much bigger journey. You’re building momentum now—don’t kill it by panicking, overengineering, or assuming scale is the next step.

Keep it simple. Talk to your users. Ship something small next week. Someone out there just clicked “Buy,” and that’s proof: you’re on to something.
