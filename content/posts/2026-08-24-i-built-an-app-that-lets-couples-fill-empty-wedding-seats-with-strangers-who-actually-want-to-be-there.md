## The App You Didn’t Know You Needed
Someone on [r/sideproject](https://www.reddit.com/r/sideproject/) built an app where couples, staring at heartbreaking rows of empty wedding chairs, can invite vetted strangers to join the festivities. Strangers who *want* to be there. That’s right: random guests.
It’s either an ingenious social experiment or an unhinged Black Mirror episode. But considering the triangle of modern wedding pain—brutal costs, last-minute RSVPs, and the socially awkward shuffle of who gets invited—it scratches an itch someone’s clearly feeling. One Redditor hilariously quipped, “This app solves a problem I didn’t think I had until... well, now.”
## How It Works: A Quick Rundown
From what the developer shared (this feels like v0.8 territory, tops), couples post the empty seats along with basic event info: date, location, vibe. Strangers apply, writing a mini pitch about why they’d be an amazing +1.
The app filters for interests, proximity, and other user-defined criteria. Matches are vetted—à la Airbnb guests—by profiles and reviews. Couples swipe “yes,” like it’s Tinder but for enthusiastic plus-ones. Then, strangers show up in their crisp cocktail attire, ready to blend into your Aunt Susan’s chaotic table.
Apparently, the app takes a cut ($20-ish per seat based on a thread comment). Or it’s free if your wedding’s totally open bar—a nice touch.
## Is This Totally Bonkers or... Kinda Brilliant?
Let’s call a spade a spade: most couples aren’t fretting over 4 empty chairs. This is overkill unless you already run in Indie Hackers circles and think **“weddings” + “optimization problem”** naturally go hand in hand.
But for niche edge cases? I get it. Maybe you’ve got 200 prepaid buffet plates, two-thirds of your flaky friends bailed last minute, and you *still* want a buzzing dance floor. Or maybe you’re an extrovert who loves the idea of unexpected connections at a deeply personal event. (Weird flex, tbh, but everyone’s wired differently.)
One Redditor summed it up perfectly: *“This is my personal introvert nightmare but also something my best friend would absolutely vibe with.”* So, yeah. It might be your thing. Or it might not.
## Alternatives: Because Nobody Said You Had to Invite Strangers
If you like the concept (filling empty seats) but aren’t ready to host random people at one of the most stressful days of your life, here’s what’s already out there:
1. **Downsize the Wedding Plan in Advance**  
   Chop your guest list *and* your expenses. Vendors like Minted or Zola let you build RSVP templates to track no-shows early, so you don’t overpay for no-eaters. Or elope entirely. **Pro move:** Host a free Zoom stream for the no-shows.
2. **Seat-Fillers, But Not Strangers**  
   This idea isn’t technically new. Big events have *seat fillers*—actors or volunteers paid to be background humans. This app just personalizes it. You can DIY this by reaching out to casual friend groups or coworkers who wouldn’t have made the primary list but wouldn’t say "no" to free cake.
3. **Local Event Groups or College Students**  
   Some folks already invite classmates or adjacent communities to weddings via Meetup and university alumni groups. The difference is you’re targeting “warm” leads, not literally anyone with a downloaded app.
## Gotchas That Could Kill the Vibe
Before downloading a stranger app for your wedding, consider these lovely stress-multipliers:  
- **Background Check Anxiety:** Airbnb vibes but without the verified ID piece? Hard nope. Nobody wants a wedding crasher with a sketchy story. 
- **Style Mismatches:** Imagine the rando in Vans and a wrinkly blazer while your crowd’s rocking custom tuxes. Awkward.
- **Cost Efficiency Isn’t Obvious:** At $20 a seat—assuming the number shared on Reddit's thread—you’ve got to compare that to just letting an empty chair vibe silently. Not every gap in photos will haunt your sleep.
## So... Would You Actually Use This?
For 99% of people planning a wedding, nah. Your emotional bandwidth is toast by month two of venue-hunting. And even if the tech works, convincing your in-laws that you invited three strangers probably won’t bring them around on that whole “we didn’t host at a church” decision.
But for indie hacker types itching for novelty? Maybe you slap this on Reddit or Twitter after the big day and move on with an absurd story for the books.
### FAQs
#### Does this kind of app already exist?  
Kind of. Several community-driven apps exist for events, but most focus on freeform mingling (think Meetup or Bumble BFF). The explicit “strangers at weddings” angle, though? That’s niche AF.
#### What’s the price breakdown on this?  
According to the developer’s post, it’s roughly $20 per stranger filled seat. But this depends on your region and whether stuff like drinks/transport are factored in.
#### Is there seriously a demand for this?  
Honestly, who knows? If your life philosophy is “there’s a subreddit for everything,” the answer is probably “yes, for some ultra-specific crowd.”
