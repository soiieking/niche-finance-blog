I spent three hours in r/personalfinance last week and one thread kept pulling me back. A guy named u/roof_repair_guy_2024 posted that he got a $480/year discount on his homeowners insurance just by calling his carrier after replacing his asphalt shingles with Class 4 impact-resistant ones.

The top comment? "Wait, you're supposed to tell them?"

Yeah. You are. And most people don't, which means they're leaving real money on the table.

## Why insurance companies actually care about your roof

Here's the thing: your roof is the single most expensive component your insurer covers. A 20-year-old roof with curling shingles is a claim waiting to happen. A brand new Class 4 roof? That's a risk reduction they can price.

Most carriers offer discounts between 5% and 25% on the dwelling portion of your premium for:

- **Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** (the big one)
- **Newer roof age** (under 10 years typically)
- **Wind mitigation features** (strapped trusses, sealed decking)
- **Metal or tile roofs** in hail-prone states

One commenter in that thread, u/texas_hail_storm_vet, said his State Farm premium dropped from $2,100 to $1,650 annually after he sent photos of his new GAF Timberline HDZ shingles. That's a 21% cut. For a phone call and two photos.

## The exact timing matters

Here's where most people screw up. They call *after* the roof is installed and the invoice is paid. That works, but you're leaving money on the table for the weeks or months between installation and your call.

The better move: **call before you sign the contract.**

Here's why. Your insurer can tell you exactly which roofing materials qualify for their discount *before* you commit. Some carriers only discount specific shingle lines. Others require a specific wind rating. If you find out after the fact that your contractor installed a shingle that doesn't qualify, you're stuck.

One guy in the thread, u/midwest_roofer_throwaway, actually named the exact shingle that got him the discount: Owens Corning Duration STORM. He said his agent told him that specific line qualified for the maximum wind credit in his state. That's the kind of intel you can only get by asking first.

## The script that works

You don't need to be a smooth talker. Here's what I'd say, verbatim:

> "Hi, I'm planning to replace my roof in the next 30 days. Before I sign the contract, I want to know what roofing materials qualify for your impact-resistant or wind mitigation discounts. Can you send me the specific requirements?"

That's it. Nine times out of ten, they'll either:

1. Send you a PDF with the exact specs, or
2. Tell you to have your contractor submit a roof certification form after installation.

## What to do after installation

Once the roof is on, you've got a small window. Most carriers want the paperwork within 60-90 days of installation. Here's your checklist:

1. **Get the manufacturer's warranty certificate** — this proves the shingle line and class rating
2. **Take photos** — wide shots of the whole roof, plus close-ups of the shingle edge showing the class rating stamp
3. **Ask your contractor for a roof certification form** — many will fill this out for free, and some states require it for the discount
4. **Call your agent** — not the 1-800 claims line. Your local agent has more flexibility to apply credits

One commenter mentioned that their contractor charged $150 for the certification form. That's annoying, but it paid for itself in about four months of premium savings. Your mileage may vary, but the math usually works.

## The catch nobody mentions

Not every roof qualifies. If you're replacing with the same basic 3-tab shingles you had before, you're probably not getting a discount. The savings come from *upgrading* to impact-resistant or Class 4 materials, which cost roughly 10-15% more upfront.

Here's the real math from the thread. u/roof_repair_guy_2024 paid $2,800 extra for Class 4 shingles over standard architectural ones. His insurance discount was $480/year. That's a 5.8-year payback period. If you're planning to stay in the house that long, it's a no-brainer. If you're selling in two years, it's probably not worth it.

I haven't tested this on every carrier, and the community is genuinely split on whether the discount is worth the upgrade cost. But the *call* costs you nothing. Even if you don't upgrade, telling your insurer about a new roof of any kind can sometimes get you a small age-based credit.

## FAQ

**Do I need to tell my insurance company if I'm just replacing with the same type of roof?**

Yes. Even a like-for-like replacement changes your roof's age, which affects your risk profile. Some carriers automatically reduce premiums for newer roofs, but only if they know about it.

**Will my premium go up if I tell them about the new roof?**

It shouldn't, unless you're in a state with weird roof depreciation rules (looking at you, Florida and Louisiana). In most cases, a new roof either lowers your premium or keeps it flat. The risk of *not* telling them is worse — if you file a claim and they discover the roof was replaced without notification, they can deny or reduce the payout.

**Can I get the discount retroactively if I already replaced my roof?**

Usually yes, as long as you have documentation. Call your agent, send photos and the warranty certificate, and ask if they can apply the credit retroactively to your last renewal date. Some carriers will, some won't. It's worth the five-minute call.
