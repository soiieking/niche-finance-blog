Nvidia's new RTX 5090 is here, and it’s a monster. 🤯 But if you’re trying to buy one in the US, you’re probably out of luck. The card practically vanished from retailers within hours of launch, and third-party sellers are now slapping absurd $9,000+ price tags on it. Is anyone *seriously* paying that? And more importantly, do you need this GPU, or is this just FOMO running wild?

Let’s break this down — from the raw numbers to the scalper mess to whether this upgrade even makes sense for your rig.

## The RTX 5090: Specs That Make Your Jaw Drop (But Do You Need Them?)

First, the basics. The RTX 5090 boasts 32GB of GDDR7 VRAM, 3.7 GHz boost clocks, and a whopping 2.5x increase in ray-tracing performance compared to the 4090. For AI folks training models or pushing 8K video renders, this is a dream card. In gaming? You’re looking at Cyberpunk 2077 with every setting cranked to max, DLSS 3.5, and consistent triple-digit FPS. Insanity.

But let’s address the elephant in the room: 90% of people don’t need this. Even a loaded 4080, which now floats around $1,100 after some price cuts, will destroy any modern game or workload you throw at it. Someone in the r/LocalLLaMA thread even joked: *“If it doesn’t help me train LLaMA 3 in under two days, what’s the point of a $10k GPU?”*

Unless you’re running heavy AI workloads or doing pro-level 3D rendering, the 5090 is overkill. For most builders, that $9,500 price tag isn’t just steep — it’s ridiculous.

## Why the Scalping? Blame Nvidia’s Stock Drops and Demand Spikes

So why can’t you buy one at MSRP? Partly, Nvidia underestimated this launch. The official $1,599 price tag was supposed to temper some of the hype, but it’s clear they didn’t ship enough units to meet demand. In the US, Newegg and Best Buy’s preorders sold out instantly. Now, eBay is clogged with listings from scalpers milking desperate buyers, asking $6,000 at best — and $9,500 at worst.

This isn’t new. We saw the same with the 3080 launch in 2020 during the pandemic (anyone else pay 2x MSRP back then because you couldn’t wait?). But scalping in 2026 is *somehow* worse because there's both insane demand from the AI crowd *and* people flexing on TikTok with $10k GPUs.

My advice? Unless you absolutely need it for work, *wait*. Nvidia’s supply chain will catch up, and these prices will settle in 2-3 months. We’ve seen similar trends with every major GPU launch from the last 5 years. Patience saves money. If you can’t wait? Plug that cash into an RTX 4080 or 4090 instead. You'll still get top-tier performance at a fraction of this insanity.

## Alternatives: Maybe It’s Time to Look Beyond Nvidia?

If you’re into gaming or creative workflows, AMD deserves a mention. Their Radeon RX 7900 XTX delivers 24GB VRAM and excellent rasterization performance for just $999 MSRP. Sure, AMD’s ray-tracing lags behind Nvidia (and it's not even close), but for workflows that don’t rely on path tracing or CUDA-specific apps? It’s a fantastic choice.

For AI workloads, though, Nvidia still owns the space — CUDA libraries and tensor cores are the standard. But *if* your projects can pivot to open standards like Vulkan or ROCm, AMD’s MI300X chips are promising alternatives. Those are definitely niche plays, but hey, if Nvidia’s ecosystem continues bleeding your wallet dry, they’re worth keeping on your radar.

## Final Thoughts: FOMO and the Scalper Tax

Look, the RTX 5090 is an absolute beast. It’s also wildly overkill for most people — and hilariously overpriced right now thanks to scalpers. Unless you’re about to lose revenue without it (and let’s be real: only like 2% of buyers *are*), wait until retail stock stabilizes. Or save $8k and pick up a 4080. Your wallet will thank you, and for every workload outside hardcore AI, you’ll barely notice the difference.

If you do drop $9,500 on one? At least post an unboxing thread and let us live vicariously through you.

### FAQ

#### **Why does Nvidia dominate AI hardware?**
CUDA is the short answer. Nvidia’s proprietary framework is the backbone of most AI workflows, and replacing it is a hassle. In contrast, AMD’s ROCm is still growing, and while it’s open-source, adoption has been slow.

#### **Will RTX 5090 prices drop soon?**
Probably. Nvidia's supply shortages typically improve within 2-3 months after launch. Look at the 4090 — scalping dropped off in about 6 weeks once more stock hit retailers.

#### **How much power does the RTX 5090 draw?**
Nominal TDP is 450W, but peak loads can push closer to 500W. Make sure your PSU can handle it; ideally, you’ll want a quality 1000W unit for headroom.
