AMD stock might not be gold bars in a Swiss vault, but high-value assets like these can easily go unclaimed when tragedy hits. Someone on r/personalfinance recently asked how to claim AMD shares still lingering under their deceased spouse's name. Fair warning: this process can be both a paperwork maze and, weirdly enough, not as scary as it sounds.

Here’s how to tackle it without skipping lunch.

## 1. Start with the Death Certificate

This is your golden key to everything. You cannot even sneeze in the world of post-mortem asset transfers without an official death certificate. Get a few certified copies (yes, certified is key). You might think one is enough—but trust me, financial institutions and transfer agents will fight over them like toddlers with the last cookie.

Pro tip: Some folks on the r/personalfinance thread suggested getting five copies because "we ran out mid-process." That problem will ruin your day, so overshoot here.

## 2. Figure Out Who Holds the Shares

Are these AMD shares from way back in the ‘90s (probably pre-7nm era)? If so, they’re likely in pure certificate form—those sexy, physical documents that feel like Monopoly money for rich people.

If the shares are modern (and digital), they’re probably held in a brokerage account. Check records, emails, or go digging through the filing cabinet of doom. If you're not sure where the shares are, AMD’s transfer agent can usually help. For AMD, this is Computershare—aka the company that handles this paper-pushing (and sometimes soul-crushing) work.

Reach out to them ASAP. They’ll ask for proof of death, ID, and possibly a copy of the will/trust before even looking in the database.

## 3. Joint Accounts Make Everything Easier

If the shares were held in a joint brokerage account (e.g., with "rights of survivorship"), congratulations—you get to dodge a huge chunk of the nightmare. Joint accounts usually allow for what’s called "survivorship rights," meaning the shares default to your name automatically.

But, and it’s a big but, you still need to talk to the custodian or broker to get the account officially switched over to your name. Bring the death certificate—always.

If your spouse's name was the only one on the account… strap in. You’re looking at probate court. Keep reading.

## 4. Prepare for Probate (if Necessary)

Probate is a dirty word in finance, but here’s the deal: If the shares weren't in a joint account or trust, you’ll probably deal with probate court to establish ownership. The court’s job is to validate the will (or assign ownership when there isn’t one). This can take weeks, if not months, depending on the complexity of the estate. People online report timelines of 2-6 months for most simple estates.

If there's a will, bring it. No will? The state laws of intestacy step in to divvy things up. This part sucks, but it’s rooted in old legal traditions for a reason. Heads up: probate court fees ain't cheap. Expect $2,000–$5,000 depending on your state.

## 5. Call in Help When You’re Stuck

Depending on how much money these shares are worth, hiring an estate attorney may save you from weeks of headache. It's overkill for shares worth, say, $1,000. But if we’re talking a portfolio worth low five figures or more, paying a lawyer their $300/hour fee can be worth it.

Someone in the thread suggested checking out policyholder services offered by Computershare directly. True to their rep, some agents are helpful, others borderline passive-aggressive. If you hate dealing with them, that attorney suddenly looks real attractive.

---

## Final Thoughts: It’s a Matter of Patience

Claiming old AMD shares tied up in your spouse's estate isn’t the fastest process. But it also doesn't need to be a Herculean task. Certificates, will/probate documents, and a good helping of patience are your big three tools here. And don’t sleep on the advice from the r/personalfinance crowd—a lot of them have been in your shoes.

It’s not fun, but it’s doable. One step at a time.

### FAQ

#### How can I check if shares are still unclaimed?

Most companies keep records going back decades—transfer agents like Computershare can help with this. You might also check the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators (NAUPA) to see if any dividends or stock accounts are listed under your late spouse.

#### What does "rights of survivorship" mean?

It means that ownership of an asset automatically passes to the surviving co-owner(s) without going through probate. It applies to joint accounts with "right of survivorship" explicitly stated in the terms.

#### What if I can't find a will?

If there’s no will, the estate goes through what’s called intestate succession. State laws decide who gets what, usually starting with the closest relatives (you, if married). This process is slower and can require extra proofs of relationship.
