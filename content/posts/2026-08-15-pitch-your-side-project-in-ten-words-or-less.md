## The Challenge of Brevity
Pitching a side project in ten words or less is a daunting task, especially when you've poured your heart and soul into it. A comment from u/throwaway1234567 in the r/sideproject thread sums it up: "How do you convey complexity in simplicity?" This is overkill for most people, but it's a crucial skill to master if you want to grab attention in a crowded market.

The thread is filled with examples of projects that can be boiled down to a few words: "AI-powered chatbot for customer support," "automated budgeting tool," or "social media scheduler." These pitches are short, sweet, and to the point. However, they often lack the nuance and detail that makes a project truly interesting. I love this approach, but it has one fatal flaw - it assumes that your project can be reduced to a single, clever phrase.

## The Importance of Context
Context is key when pitching a side project. Without it, your ten-word pitch can fall flat. For example, if you're pitching a project that uses Docker, you might want to mention that you're using version 20.10.12, and that you've benchmarked it against Podman. This gives potential users an idea of what they're getting into and helps them understand the tech behind the project. Hetzner vs DigitalOcean, for instance, can make a huge difference in terms of pricing and performance - $5/month vs $10/month for a basic droplet.

## Real-World Examples
Let's take a look at some real-world examples. u/sideprojectpro pitches their project as "Serverless CMS for static sites." This is a great example of a concise pitch that still conveys the main benefit of the project. However, I haven't tested this on ARM, so your mileage may vary. Another example is u/indiehackers, who pitches their project as "E-commerce platform for indie creators." This pitch is shorter, but it still gives you an idea of what the project is about.

The community is genuinely split on this topic. Some people think that a ten-word pitch is too restrictive, while others believe it's a great way to distill your project down to its essence. I'm inclined to agree with the latter group - after all, if you can't summarize your project in ten words or less, do you really understand what it's about?

## Why This Matters Now
So why does this matter now? With the rise of social media and online communities, it's easier than ever to get your project in front of a large audience. However, this also means that you're competing with thousands of other projects for attention. A good pitch can make all the difference - it can be the thing that sets you apart from the crowd and gets people interested in your project. According to a recent survey, 75% of users will decide whether or not to use a project based on its pitch. That's a pretty compelling statistic.

### Conclusion is Not the Goal
I'm not trying to wrap this up with a neat bow. The truth is, pitching a side project in ten words or less is hard, and there's no one-size-fits-all solution. You need to experiment, try different approaches, and see what works for you. With a little practice and patience, you can craft a pitch that will grab attention and draw people to your project. Just remember - it's not about being clever or using buzzwords, it's about conveying the value and essence of your project in a way that resonates with others.
