OP's situation is not unique - they're drowning under the financial burden of taking in a teenager. One user, u/throwaway12345678, summed it up: "I'm not asking for financial help, I'm asking for a miracle." 
This is overkill for most people, but for those who can relate, here's what the community has to say.
### Financial Planning for the Unplanned
When taking in a teenager, it's essential to reassess your budget. As u/frugalfox pointed out, "The 50/30/20 rule is a good starting point, but you'll need to adjust it to account for the additional expenses." 
One way to do this is by using the "envelope system." Allocate specific amounts for groceries, entertainment, and other expenses, and stick to it. This can help you avoid overspending and make the most of your money.
### Managing Expenses with Budgeting Tools
Several users recommended using budgeting tools like YNAB (You Need a Budget) or Mint to track expenses and stay on top of finances. However, as u/brokebutmakingit noted, "YNAB can be a bit pricey, especially if you're on a tight budget." 
A more affordable alternative is Personal Capital, which offers a free version with limited features. If you're comfortable with spreadsheets, Google Sheets can also be a great option.
### Cutting Expenses and Finding Savings
To make ends meet, it's crucial to identify areas where you can cut expenses and find savings. One user, u/savvy_sally, suggested, "Cancel subscription services you don't use, like streaming services or gym memberships." 
Another user, u/frugalfrank, recommended using the 30-day rule: "When you see something you want to buy, wait 30 days before making a purchase." This can help you determine if the item is something you truly need or just a impulsive want.
### The Importance of Emergency Funds
Having an emergency fund in place can be a lifesaver when taking in a teenager. As u/financialfreedom123 noted, "Aim to save 3-6 months' worth of expenses in an easily accessible savings account." 
This fund can help you cover unexpected expenses, such as medical bills or car repairs, without going into debt.
### Final Thoughts
Taking in a teenager can be a challenging and expensive experience. However, with the right mindset and tools, it's possible to manage your finances and make the most of the situation. As u/thriftythomas said, "It's not about being perfect; it's about being better than you were yesterday."
