## "Self-Hosting Everything": A Reality Check from r/{subreddit}
### Short on Ammo
brutusable, a regular on r/selfhosted, recently proposed a bold idea: "why not self-host every single thing?" Their argument? "It's 2023, indexing is cheap, and I'm pretty sure I can handle a few extra requests."
### The Truth Hurts
Unfortunately, reality has a few things to say about this notion. Take security, for instance. Even with the best security measures, there's always a risk of compromise. In 2021, a vulnerability in the official Docker Hub exposed millions of images, potentially allowing attackers to hijack other users' Docker accounts. selfshominated, a veteran of the containerization wars, summed it up: "Your Docker Hub token is probably in the same password manager as your admin password."
### The Obvious Obstacles
Then there's the issue of convenience. While some services require minimal setup, others demand a significant investment in time and resources. Take Voicemeeter, for example. Popular on r/linuxmusic, Voicemeeter is a robust audio and MIDI routing tool. However, its users often lament its lengthy installation process and extensive configuration options. As voicecash puts it: “If your mic isn’t working, congratulations, you’ve just proved you do indeed have the balls for self-hosting.”
### The Elephant in the Room
Last but certainly not least, there's the issue of money. In the real world, cost is often a major factor. A self-hosted service might offer more control, but it can't always match the affordability of a cloud-based solution.
