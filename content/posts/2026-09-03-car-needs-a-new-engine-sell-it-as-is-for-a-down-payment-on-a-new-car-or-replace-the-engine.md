## The Big Question: Fix It or Sell It?

Your car’s engine is toast. No weird hacks, no miracle additives, nothing. You're down to two options: spend money on a new engine or sell the car as-is and put that cash into something else. It’s a tough spot a *ton* of people on r/personalfinance end up in.

Let’s figure this out.  

Spoiler: The smarter choice depends on how much your car is worth, what the repair costs, and your wider life situation. There’s no "one-size-fits-all" here, but I can help you get close to the right answer.

---

## Quick Math: The Two Most Important Numbers

First, grab two numbers:

1. **What’s your car worth after the repair?** Use Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com) or Edmunds to look up its private party value assuming it runs. Be realistic about mileage, age, and features.

2. **What’s an engine replacement going to cost you?** Call up a mechanic—or three—and ask. Don’t skip this. Engine swaps usually range between **$4,000 and $8,000**, depending on your car and whether you’re okay with a rebuilt engine. Dealerships will charge the high end, indie shops will usually be cheaper. You can only know for sure by asking.

Now subtract the cost of repair from the car’s post-repair value. If the car is worth $10,000 after a $6,000 repair, you’re left with $4,000 of “gain.” If repairing leaves you underwater, throw this idea in the trash immediately.

This math is where most people stop, but it’s not everything. Don’t skip the next sections—they matter.

---

## What’s Your Current Car Worth As-Is?

Seriously, don’t guess.

Even if the engine’s shot, your car isn’t worth $0. Salvage buyers, mechanics, or even DIY folks will pay cash for it. Use Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, or CarBrain to list it as-is. One commenter on r/personalfinance sold their car with an engine failure for **$3,500**. (Do not assume this is typical, though—expect maybe $500 to $2,000 for most dead cars.)

This number matters. It’s your “baseline down payment” on the next car.

---

## Replacement Cars Are Expensive Right Now

This sucks, but it’s 2026 and car prices are still bananas. Even a certified pre-owned Toyota Corolla or Honda Civic is probably $22k. Financing that means running into current car loan rates, which are around **8-10% APR** unless your credit is exceptional.

Do you really want a $400/month payment in your life right now? If you’re already scraping by or paying off high-interest credit cards, patching up the current car may actually be cheaper in the long run—even if the upfront cost hurts. 

To decide, look at **your full financial picture.** This is where personal finance gets annoyingly personal.

---

## When Fixing the Car Makes Sense

- **You love the car.** Maybe it has sentimental value, or you prefer the devil you know. If your current ride is something like a 2015 Subaru Outback you’ve maintained well (beyond the engine, duh), fixing it might make sense.
  
- **It beats new loan payments.** Sinking $6k into repairs isn’t fun, but it’s still cheaper than taking on $25k of new debt.

- **You’ve already replaced major components.** If you’ve had the transmission or suspension rebuilt recently, fixing the engine could give you another 100k+ miles before it needs big-ticket repairs again.

That said, don’t repair it if the car’s a lemon in general. A fresh engine won’t save a vehicle with rust holes big enough to store a sandwich.

---

## When Selling As-Is Is Smarter

- **The car’s junk beyond the engine.** If your car also needs a new transmission, struts, and tires... just stop. Sell it as a parts car and walk away. No use pouring $6,000 into a $2,500 mess.

- **You’ve been wanting something new anyway.** Be honest—did you already hate the car before the engine died? This repair might be your sign to cut bait.

- **You need cash more than a car right now.** Let’s not pretend everyone’s rolling in savings. If selling the car as-is gets you enough to buy a reliable beater without taking out a loan, do it. Cars like an early-2010s Lexus ES can often be found cheap and will last forever.

## FAQ

### How much is a used engine?

Rebuilt engines usually cost **$2,000-$4,000** before labor. Labor adds another **$1,500-$3,000**, depending on your mechanic and car. If it’s a BMW or Audi? Double it.  

### Are there cheap financing options for engine replacements?

Some mechanics offer payment plans or 0% financing promotions. But read the fine print—rate hikes or fees can kick in hard if you miss payments.

### Can I install the engine myself?

If you know your way around an engine hoist and have a lot of time, sure. But for most people, this is a nightmare job best left to pros. Your call.

---

At the end of the day, it’s about numbers and your own situation. Be honest with yourself, run the math, and remember: cars are just tools. The one that costs you the least long-term is the better choice.
