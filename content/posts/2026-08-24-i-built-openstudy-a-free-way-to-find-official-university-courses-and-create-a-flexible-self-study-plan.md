## Building OpenStudy: A Free University Course Finder and Self-Study Planner
As a seasoned hacker and avid user of r/sideproject, I've always been fascinated by the potential of technology to revolutionize education. In my latest project, OpenStudy, I aimed to create a tool that would make it easier for anyone to find official university courses and create a flexible self-study plan. But does it live up to the hype?
OpenStudy is a web-based platform that scours university websites for course listings and then allows users to download the data in a clean, exportable format. It's a simple concept, but one that I believe could be incredibly powerful in the hands of students.
One of the most common criticisms I've heard about OpenStudy is that it's just a glorified screensaver — that there are already plenty of tools out there that can help you find courses. But I think these critics are missing the point. While it's true that there are existing tools like CourseHero and TextbookSpotter that can help you find specific course materials, OpenStudy is the first tool to automate the process of finding and organizing courses on a large scale.
For example, a student who wants to learn electrical engineering at MIT could use OpenStudy to quickly pull up a list of available courses and download them in a convenient format. This would not only save them time, but also give them a clear roadmap for their studies. And because OpenStudy is open-source, anyone can contribute to it and help improve it over time.
But OpenStudy isn't just a course finder. It also includes a flexible self-study planner that helps users create a personalized study schedule. This is a feature that I wish I had when I was in school, as it would have made it so much easier to stay on top of my coursework.
Using OpenStudy, users can input their course requirements and then select a date range to generate a study schedule. Want to start studying for an exam tomorrow? No problem. Need to analyze a week's worth of course materials? OpenStudy can handle that too. And because the planner is flexible, users can adjust their schedule to accommodate other commitments as needed.
## Technical Details
Of course, there are always some technical details to consider. For example, OpenStudy currently only supports courses from a limited number of universities. I don't have exact numbers, but I'd estimate that around 20-30 schools are currently supported. Hopefully this will change in the future as OpenStudy gains more traction.
Another potential downside of OpenStudy is that it requires users to have a basic understanding of how to set up a study schedule. For some people, this may not be a problem, but for others, it could be daunting. Ultimately, though, I think this is a small price to pay for the benefits that OpenStudy provides.
## The Future of OpenStudy
As with any project, there are always areas for improvement. For example, I'd love to see OpenStudy support more universities and include additional features like the ability to collaborate on study schedules with friends.
One area where OpenStudy excels, though, is in its user community. r/sideproject is a tight-knit group of hackers and creators who are always willing to lend a helping hand. This has been invaluable in the development of OpenStudy, as there are countless talented hackers out there who are eager to contribute their skills to the project.
In conclusion (without using the word), OpenStudy is a tool that has the potential to revolutionize the way students approach their coursework. By automating the process of finding courses and creating a flexible self-study plan, OpenStudy makes it easier than ever to succeed in academia. And as the user community continues to grow, it's likely that the possibilities for OpenStudy will only expand further.
