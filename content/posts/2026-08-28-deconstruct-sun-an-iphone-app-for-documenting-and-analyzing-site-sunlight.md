## What Is Deconstruct Sun?
Deconstruct Sun is an iPhone app that does one thing: tells you exactly how a given location will be hit by sunlight throughout the year. It's ostensibly for people planning their dream gardens, solar panel placements, or anything where knowing "how much sun and when" matters. Snap a photo, enable some site data, and boom—a sunlight heatmap overlaid on your target scene.
The big idea is this: sunlight isn’t static or simple. Seasons shift, shadows fall, and angles get weird. Deconstruct Sun tries to simplify all of that so you can make smarter design decisions for places like patios, rooftops, or even your workstation by the window. 
It seems niche—and let’s be real, it is—but this app hits a growing sweet spot.
## Why Now?
Timing, both literally and metaphorically, is key here. 
First, everyone’s hyper-aware of climate right now. Energy efficiency, sustainable design, and even stuff like maximizing passive solar heating in small builds are no longer just for the architect-nerds. They’re creeping into mainstream homeowner conversations. 
Second, the tools for this used to suck. A comment on r/sideproject nailed it: "We'd hack this stuff together in SketchUp with plugin packs and then still have to guess." That’s true. Pre-2020, this kind of analysis needed ugly pro tools or wild amounts of patience. The rise of a mobile app that makes it effortless? That’s a 2020s development.
Third, the app-store economy is shifting toward hyper-focused utilities. The golden age of broad "do it all" apps like Wunderlist is long dead. People (okay, some people) are willing to pay $9.99 or even $19.99 for an app that solves one big complex problem *really well*. Solar predictions? Sure.
## The Good Stuff 
### Hyper-Specialized With Simple UX
The best pitch? It doesn’t *feel* like 3D modeling software. You snap a photo, adjust a couple markers, and the app crunches seasonal sunlight data into a thing you can actually use. 
People in the subreddit raved about the workflow. One user said, "It took me 15 minutes to figure out ideal placement for my garden beds—and I’m terrible at tech." At its core, it’s solving complex problems without making you kill half a Saturday Googling.
### Accurate (But Not Magic)
This works because of the existing astronomical libraries and APIs it piggybacks on. After all, the sun’s path is a well-known quantity. Most users report the overlays align *really well* with real-world results—give or take a one-off bug on uneven terrain (this doesn’t replace real surveying, btw). 
Side note: if someone makes a lightweight LiDAR add-on for this, it’d be game over.
## The Weak Spots
### Overkill for Most Folks
Here’s the flip side: for 99% of people, you could approximate the same results by lurking in a spot for a week with a notebook and your eyeballs. Unless you’re literally landscaping, designing a tiny home, or planning solar panels, it might feel like an intellectual flex more than a *necessary* tool. 
(Bonus: the people who *do* need this probably already have design firms handling it. Which makes me wonder if this app’s market is more aspirational than utilitarian.)
### No Android Love (Yet)  
As of writing, this is iPhone-only. That’s a dealbreaker for plenty of folks. The team hasn’t straight-up ruled out Android support, but the roadmap is unclear. If you’re a Samsung-type waiting to analyze your yard, you’ll just have to wait—or find an alternative.
## What’s This Mean For Indie-Hackers?
I’m not saying you should pivot your app into solar mapping, but this project scratches an itch a lot of us should pay attention to: highly-niche tools that make old pro workflows *dumb easy* for normals. 
Think about it. 15 years ago, nobody predicted household-level CAD or that we’d buy $400 iPads to doodle floorplans. Markets are weird like that. Apps like Deconstruct Sun are pulling down another chunk of expertise from pros-only to DIY-ready. All with an indie budget. 
## FAQ
### Who is this app for, really?
Optimists with a very specific goal: maximizers of outdoor space, folks planning solar setups, and anyone designing their dream garden. Bonus points if you’re outside the “throw money at professionals” income tier.
### How accurate is it compared to pro tools?  
Based on user reviews (and a lot of astronomy math), it’s shockingly close for a $10 app—but it’s not a replacement for full-on site surveying for things like legal regulations. If you’re building a $50k solar array, maybe still consult a pro.
### Any free alternatives to this?
Kinda. You can cobble together something like this using a combo of SketchUp plugins and public sunlight path calculators…but it’s way more effort. Or you can try free AR apps like Sun Seeker, though they’re less detailed.
