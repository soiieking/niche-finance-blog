I feel you, OP – I've been there too. In fact, I've built and broken a few things in my time, and I've come to realize that DIY estate planning is a recipe for disaster.

## The DIY Trap

Let's face it: estate planning is not for the faint of heart. It involves navigating a complex web of laws, taxes, and bureaucratic red tape. And yet, many people try to tackle it themselves, either out of ignorance or a misguided sense of thriftiness. I mean, who needs a lawyer when you've got the internet, right?

Not me, that's for sure. I've seen too many people get burned by DIY estate planning. Take, for example, the case of the OP who tried to use a free online will generator. Sounds like a good idea, right? Wrong. As one commenter pointed out, "I used one of those online will generators and it ended up costing me $10,000 in probate fees." Yeah, that's not exactly what I'd call a "free" service.

## The Cost of DIY

So, what's the cost of DIY estate planning, exactly? Well, let's take a look at some numbers. According to a survey by the American Bar Association, the average cost of probate in the United States is around $10,000. That's not including the cost of preparing the will, which can range from $500 to $2,000, depending on the complexity of the estate.

And then there's the time factor. Estate planning can be a full-time job, especially if you're dealing with a complex estate. As one commenter pointed out, "I've been working on my dad's estate for months, and I'm still not done. It's taking up all my free time and energy." That's not exactly what I'd call a "free" activity.

## When to Call in the Professionals

So, when do you know it's time to call in the professionals? For me, it's when the DIY approach starts to get in the way of my actual job. And that's exactly what's happening with the OP. They're spending so much time dealing with their dad's estate that they're starting to neglect their own work.

As one commenter pointed out, "I think the OP needs to take a step back and prioritize their own life. Estate planning is not a DIY project – it's a full-time job." Amen to that.

## The Bottom Line

So, what's the takeaway here? For me, it's that DIY estate planning is a recipe for disaster. It's a complex, time-consuming, and expensive process that's best left to the professionals. And if you're not willing to pay for a lawyer, then maybe you shouldn't be doing estate planning in the first place.

As one commenter pointed out, "If you can't afford to pay for a lawyer, then maybe you shouldn't be doing estate planning at all." Harsh, but true.


### FAQ

*Q: What's the best way to get started with estate planning?*
A: Start by consulting with a lawyer or a financial advisor who specializes in estate planning. They can help you navigate the complex process and ensure that your estate is handled correctly.

*Q: How much does estate planning cost?*
A: The cost of estate planning can vary widely, depending on the complexity of the estate and the services required. However, on average, you can expect to pay between $500 and $2,000 for a will, and $10,000 or more for probate.

*Q: Can I use an online will generator to save money?*
A: No, it's not recommended. While online will generators may seem like a cost-effective option, they can often lead to costly mistakes and complications down the line. It's better to err on the side of caution and consult with a lawyer or financial advisor instead.
